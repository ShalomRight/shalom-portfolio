# Shalom Sutherland — Brand Tone & Voice Guide

## Who You Are
You're a designer who moves across disciplines without losing specificity. Brand identity, motion graphics, UI/UX, interactive web — you don't dabble, you execute. Caribbean-based, globally fluent. Your work has weight behind it. This portfolio communicates that without over-explaining it.

---

## Voice Pillars

### 1. Precise, Not Wordy
You say the meaningful thing and stop. No filler, no over-qualification, no "I really enjoyed working on this." Every sentence earns its place.

> ❌ "This was a really fun and challenging project where I got to explore a lot of different design directions."
> ✅ "Built to move fast and feel premium. The challenge was both."

### 2. Confident, Not Arrogant
You know your craft. You don't hedge it. But you're not performing — you're just clear.

> ❌ "I think this turned out pretty well."
> ✅ "The final system scaled to 12 brand touchpoints."

### 3. Designer Talking to Designers (and Clients)
You write as someone who understands both sides of the table — the creative and the strategic. Use proper design language where it adds meaning, not to sound smart.

### 4. Kinetic Energy
Your copy has rhythm. Short sentences hit. Longer ones carry context. The read should feel like your work moves.

## Tone by Section

| Section | Tone |
|---|---|
| Hero / Intro | Sparse. Direct. A statement, not a bio. |
| Project Header | Sharp. Sets the stage in 1–2 lines. |
| Case Study Body | Informative but alive — no dry recapping. |
| Process Notes | Honest. Show thinking, not just outcomes. |
| Logo/Brand Work | Visual-forward. Let the work lead. |
| About | First-person, grounded, no fluff. |

---

## Writing Style Rules

- **No passive voice** unless it specifically serves the sentence.
- **Short paragraphs.** 2–3 sentences max per block.
- **No buzzwords** — "innovative," "seamless," "cutting-edge" are banned.
- **Numbers anchor claims.** If you can say "3-week sprint" instead of "a short timeline," do it.
- **Lead with the work, not the process.** Start with what was made.

## Logo / Signature Direction

**Concept:** Your name as a signature mark — not a constructed logomark. Think written identity, not icon.

**Direction:**
- Script or calligraphic treatment of "Shalom Sadler"
- High contrast: thick-to-thin stroke variation
- One ligature or connecting flourish between first and last name
- Works as a standalone horizontal lockup
- Scales down to a monogram "SS" for favicons and stamps

**Feel:** Editorial. Personal. The kind of signature you'd see on editorial illustrations or luxury motion credits.

**What to avoid:** Anything that looks like a "logo generator" sans-serif with a geometric shape beside it.

## Color & Aesthetic Direction (Starting Point)

Develop this further once work samples are in — but directionally:

- **Dark-mode first.** Deep background (not pure black — a very dark warm gray or near-black blue).
- **One accent color** — something distinct. Consider deep amber, electric cream, or cool celadon. Not red. Not purple.
- **Monochrome base** with one accent doing all the heavy lifting.
- **Typography:** Mix a refined serif display face with a clean geometric or grotesque body. Something like Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans or Editorial New + Suisse Int'l.
